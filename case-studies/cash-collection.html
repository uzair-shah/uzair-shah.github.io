Case study

Cash collection: closing out the delivery day

Making end-of-day cash deposits faster to record and easier to check.

Retailo · Operations · End-of-day cash and returns

Target audience

  • Delivery agents, who bring back the day's cash and any returned stock.
  • Cash counter staff, who take and record deposits.
  • Dock executives and stock sorters, who check returned stock.
  • The logistics team, who resolve any gap between cash expected and cash deposited.

Customer pain points

  • Every deposit was entered into a long table by hand, which was slow, easy to get wrong and kept agents waiting in line.
  • The cash an agent deposited did not always match the cash the logistics app said they had collected.
  • Returns were a common source of gaps: too few returned could mean a weak morning check, stock dropped off by mistake or theft; too many usually meant a partial delivery was marked as complete, so the cash came up short.

Clear objectives

  • Get every agent's cash deposited and recorded quickly at the end of the day.
  • Make recording a deposit fast and consistent, with no long table to scroll through.
  • Catch cash and returns mismatches and send them to the right team to resolve.

My role

  • I mapped the end-of-day cash and returns process from arrival to final check, including where mismatches came from.
  • I built a simple Google Sheets entry form for deposits: it writes each entry into the shared table on its own and clears itself, ready for the next agent.

The MVP

  • A form on top of the Google Sheet the team already used, automated with Apps Script. No new app, no training on new software, and it worked from day one.

The agent's end-of-day flow

  1. 1
    Agent returns to the warehouseDelivery agent

    Comes back at the end of the route with the day's cash, and either no stock left or some returns.

    If there are returnsThe van docks at the gate, where an executive checks the agent's returns slip against the physical stock and files it with the morning sheet.
  2. 2
    Count the cashDelivery agent

    Takes paper and rubber bands from the deposit counter, sorts the cash by note, and writes down the total for each.

  3. 3
    Cash deposit machineDelivery agent

    Enters their agent app ID and deposits the cash. The machine shows how much it accepted and hands back any notes it rejects.

  4. 4
    Deposit counterAgent and counter staff

    The agent queues and deposits the rejected notes. The counter logs the deposit in the Google Sheets form, which saves it to the shared table and clears itself for the next agent.

  5. 5
    Check against the appLogistics team

    The cash deposited is compared with the cash the logistics app says the agent collected.

    If the amounts don't matchThe logistics team decides whether the agent is genuinely short or the system made a mistake.

What made it work

A form, not a long table

Staff fill in a few fields instead of finding the right row in a long table. The sheet records the entry and resets the form, so the next agent can be served straight away.

Machine first, counter second

The deposit machine takes most of the cash and records it against the agent's app ID, so only the notes it rejects go to the counter queue.

Check returns at the dock

Returned stock is checked against the agent's slip as soon as the van arrives, which is where short and over returns show up.

Separate real shortfalls from system errors

A mismatch goes to the logistics team, who decide whether the agent is short or the app recorded the wrong amount, so agents are not blamed for system errors.

Risks

  • Over-returns hiding partial deliveries: the returns record from the dock made these visible, since they showed up as cash shortfalls.
  • On-the-spot discounts given through customer care did not fit the flow; the plan was to phase them out rather than build around them.
  • Typing errors and overwritten rows: a fixed form with the same fields every time, cleared after each entry.

Outcome

Deposits were logged through a short form instead of a long table, every entry landed in the shared sheet automatically, and the form was ready for the next agent straight away. Mismatches had a clear path to the logistics team for resolution.
